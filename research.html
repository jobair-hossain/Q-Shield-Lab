01 / Research pillar

Health informatics

How can AI make a cancer diagnosis more transparent and dependable?

We integrate medical imaging, clinical data, and multiple learning models — classical and quantum — to improve disease prediction, tumor classification, and clinical decision support. A diagnosis involves more than a single prediction: our work studies how evidence, model consensus, and interpretability can make that prediction trustworthy.

Multimodal medical data analysis for precision healthcare

Integrating whole-slide imaging, clinical data, and machine learning to improve tumor classification and decision support, including vision-transformer models that distinguish cancer, tumor, mitosis, red blood cells, and karyorrhexis in whole-slide images.

Evidence-grounded generative AI for clinical decision support

Fusion-augmented large language models that boost diagnostic trustworthiness via model consensus, and benchmarking LLMs on the USMLE for clinical reasoning and licensing scenarios.

Sentiment analysis in patient–clinician interactions

Transformer-based models that analyze sentiment in patient–clinician communication to improve healthcare experience and communication quality.

Non-invasive human hygiene monitoring

An IoT-based framework using machine learning for non-invasive human hygiene monitoring — recognized with a Best Paper Award at IEEE ICHI 2022.

02 / Research pillar

Quantum machine learning

Where does quantum learning offer a genuine advantage over strong classical baselines?

Our dissertation and lab research develop hybrid classical–quantum machine learning frameworks and benchmark them rigorously against classical methods — for biomedical analysis and for cybersecurity — with attention to which quantum optimizers and circuit designs actually help.

Hybrid classical–quantum tumor mutation classification

A comprehensive study of quantum optimizers in medical image analysis, developed as part of doctoral dissertation research on adaptive multimodal fusion for histopathological tumor analysis.

Reinforcement learning-guided adaptive fusion

Hybrid-parametrized quantum circuits combined with reinforcement learning-guided fusion for robust histopathological tumor classification.

Quantum machine learning for cybersecurity

Evaluating quantum neural networks and quantum support vector machines for malware and ransomware classification, and for detecting software supply-chain vulnerabilities in source code — directly compared against traditional machine learning baselines.

03 / Research pillar

Cybersecurity & emerging defense

How do we secure research data, software supply chains, and cyberinfrastructure at scale?

As a 2026–2027 NSF Trusted CI Fellow, the lab director works within the NSF Cybersecurity Center of Excellence to advance secure research cyberinfrastructure. This pillar spans software supply-chain security, privacy-preserving authentication, and secure scientific data access.

NSF Trusted CI Fellowship

Specialized training in research cybersecurity and secure cyberinfrastructure, culminating in a white paper and presentation at the NSF Cybersecurity Summit.

Software supply-chain security

Detecting vulnerabilities and attacks in software supply chains using both classical and quantum-enhanced machine learning approaches.

Privacy-preserving authentication for scientific data

Blockchain-based decentralized verifiable credentials and a comparative analysis of SciTokens, verifiable credentials, and smart contracts for secure access to scientific data (developed as an NCSA SciAuth Fellow).

Blockchain-based secure healthcare infrastructure

Decentralized, privacy-preserving architectures for healthcare data management and remote patient monitoring, comparing Hyperledger Fabric and Ethereum frameworks.

04 / Research pillar

Intelligent software & accessibility

How can AI-assisted software analysis and accessible design broaden who can build and trust software?

This pillar connects AI-oriented software engineering research with accessible, research-integrated computing education — including mentored undergraduate projects that turn lab methods into student-led publications.

AI-oriented software engineering

Studying the challenges, opportunities, and new directions created as AI reshapes software engineering practice, including project-based learning that integrates blockchain-oriented repositories into the curriculum.

CodePulse

A CodeBERT-validated hybrid framework that goes beyond raw LLM predictions for reliable Python bug detection — led by undergraduate researchers.

CursorVision

An AI-driven, real-time gaze-controlled framework for accessible STEM learning and educational gaming — led by undergraduate researchers.

Deep learning for object detection and tracking

CNN-based frameworks for parcel and package tracking on conveyor belts, including tools for generating robust human- and machine-annotated ground-truth training data.
